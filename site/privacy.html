Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 September 2026

The short version. Reminth has no servers, no accounts of its own, and no analytics. Nothing you do in the launcher is reported back to us, because there is nowhere for it to be reported to. Everything the launcher stores stays on your own computer, and you can delete all of it by deleting one folder.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Reminth website and the Reminth launcher for Windows. Reminth is a free, independent project. It is not affiliated with Mojang Studios, Microsoft, Modrinth or the Fabric project.

The website

The site is a static page. It sets no cookies, uses no local storage, runs no analytics, no trackers and no advertising, and has no forms — there is nothing on it that collects your name, your email address or anything else about you.

Two third parties are involved in serving it, and you should know about both:

  • Google Fonts — the main page loads two typefaces from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com. Loading them means your browser makes a request to Google, which necessarily reveals your IP address and browser to Google. This page and the Terms page deliberately load no external resources at all.
  • GitHub — the download button points at a GitHub release. Downloading the installer is a request to GitHub's servers and is subject to GitHub's own privacy policy. We receive nothing from it; we can't see who downloaded what.

Whoever hosts the site can also see standard web server request logs (IP address, time, page requested), as any web server can. That is not used to build a profile of you and is not combined with anything else.

The launcher

What it stores, and where

Everything Reminth saves lives in one folder on your computer: %APPDATA%\Reminth. That includes:

  • Your sign-in tokens, in account.json, so you don't have to sign in every time you play. On Windows these are encrypted with your own Windows account's key, using the operating system's own protection (DPAPI, via Electron's safeStorage). They never leave your machine.
  • Your launcher settings — memory, window size, accent colour and so on.
  • A cached copy of your skin, so your face still shows when you're offline.
  • A local catalogue of public Modrinth listings — mod names, authors, descriptions, download counts and icon addresses. This is public information about mods, not information about you.
  • Minecraft itself, plus the Fabric loader, Java and your worlds, in the same folder.

What it reads from your computer

To fill in the Home and Player Statistics pages, Reminth reads your worlds, your server list, your play statistics and your game logs from its own game folder. This is read-only and entirely local. None of it is uploaded anywhere, including to us.

What it sends, and to whom

Reminth contains no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking of any kind. There is no Reminth server for it to talk to. It only makes the requests it needs to do its job, and every one of them goes directly to the company that runs that service:

  • Microsoft and Xbox Live — to sign you in to your own Microsoft account.
  • Minecraft Services and Mojang — to verify your Minecraft licence, look up your profile and fetch your skin.
  • Mojang's download servers — to download the game files, which are checked against Mojang's own published hashes.
  • Fabric — to download the Fabric mod loader.
  • Modrinth's public API — to browse and search mods. These requests are anonymous: they carry no account information and nothing that identifies you.
  • Microsoft's Java download — to fetch the Java runtime the game needs.

Each of those services has its own privacy policy, which governs what they do with the request. We have no visibility into any of it.

Reminth talks to Mojang's own endpoints for your skin, not to a third-party avatar service. That is a deliberate choice: routing it elsewhere would tell a stranger's server which players use this launcher.

Children

Playing Minecraft requires a Microsoft account, and Microsoft applies its own age requirements and parental controls when you sign in. Reminth does not create an account of its own, does not ask for your age, and collects nothing directly from anyone.

Deleting your data

Sign out in the launcher to delete the stored account file. To remove everything Reminth has ever written, delete the %APPDATA%\Reminth folder — note that this also deletes any worlds you played inside Reminth, so back those up first if you want to keep them.

There is no server-side copy of anything to request, correct or export, because there is no server. If a future version of Reminth ever does store something off your machine — server hosting is the obvious candidate — that will come with its own clear notice and its own policy, not a quiet edit to this page.

Changes to this policy

If what the launcher does changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. The rule the project holds itself to is simple: this page describes what the code actually does.

Contact

Questions, corrections, or a security issue to report: open an issue on the Reminth GitHub repository. For anything you'd rather not post publicly — a security vulnerability in particular — please mark it clearly and it'll be handled privately.
